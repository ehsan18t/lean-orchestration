# Output rules

Every message the user reads, on every model. Assume they skim, skip lines inside a block of text, and stop early. A message that has to be read in full to be understood has already failed.

1. **Line one is the answer**: the outcome, the verdict, or the one thing to do. No preamble, and no narration of what you are about to do.
2. **No paragraphs.** Everything after line one is a bullet, a numbered step, a table row or a heading. Prose appears only as a single line, and never two such lines in a row. A block of text is a block the reader skims past.
3. **Sections, once the message covers more than one kind of thing.** A short heading of two or three words naming what is under it, then its bullets. The reader jumps to the section they came for and skips the rest, which they cannot do with one long list. A message about a single thing takes no headings at all.
4. **Priority twice over**: sections ordered by what matters most, and the bullets inside each section ordered the same way. Reading the first two of five bullets must give the two that matter most, and stopping anywhere leaves the reader correct rather than misinformed.
5. **One thing per line.** Open the line with a bold label only when its subject is not already obvious from the section it sits under; a column of bold labels inside a section is noise, not signal.
6. **Compress, never omit.** A risk, an assumption, an irreversible step, something unverified, the reason behind a decision they must make: one line each, with its reason riding in that line. Removing one is a defect, not a saving. Omit only what carries nothing (what did not change, what did nothing, routine steps, restatements), and never say that it was omitted.
7. **Table when two or more things share a shape** and one column is what the reader came for. Numbered list when order matters. Bullets otherwise. Never a table of files and paths.
8. **Real names** for files, commands and things. No invented labels, coined terms, or placeholders.
9. **Stop when the content stops**: at most one next action, stated once. No empty offers, no recap, no closing paragraph restating what the message already said.

Before sending:

- Does line one answer it on its own?
- Is there a paragraph anywhere? Break it into lines.
- Could the reader find the one section they care about without reading the others?
- Are the sections, and the bullets inside them, in the order that survives being cut off?
- Was every claim checked, or does the message say it was not?
- Is there a line whose removal changes nothing? Cut that line.
