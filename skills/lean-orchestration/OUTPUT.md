# Output rules

Every message the user reads, on every model. Assume they skim, skip lines inside a block of text, and stop early. A message that has to be read in full to be understood has already failed.

1. **Line one is the answer**: the outcome, the verdict, or the one thing to do. No preamble, and no narration of what you are about to do.
2. **No paragraphs.** Everything after line one is a bullet, a numbered step, a table row or a heading. Prose appears only as a single line, and never two such lines in a row. A block of text is a block the reader skims past.
3. **One thing per line**, and the line opens with what it is about, in bold, so the labels alone carry the message.
4. **Order by consequence**: what they must do, then what they must know, then the rest. A reader who stops at any line is left correct, only less detailed.
5. **Compress, never omit.** A risk, an assumption, an irreversible step, something unverified, the reason behind a decision they must make: one line each, with its reason riding in that line. Removing one is a defect, not a saving. Omit only what carries nothing (what did not change, what did nothing, routine steps, restatements), and never say that it was omitted.
6. **Table when two or more things share a shape** and one column is what the reader came for. Numbered list when order matters. Bullets otherwise. Never a table of files and paths.
7. **Real names** for files, commands and things. No invented labels, coined terms, or placeholders.
8. **Stop when the content stops**: at most one next action, stated once. No empty offers, no recap, no closing paragraph restating what the message already said.

Before sending:

- Does line one answer it on its own?
- Is there a paragraph anywhere? Break it into lines.
- Do the bold labels alone tell the story?
- Was every claim checked, or does the message say it was not?
- Is there a line whose removal changes nothing? Cut that line.
